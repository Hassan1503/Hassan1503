Md Hassanuzzaman

PhD Candidate · Machine learning for physiological signals

I build methods that turn raw physiological recordings into reliable clinical decisions. My thesis develops automated screening for congenital heart disease from heart sounds, designed to hold up on inexpensive stethoscopes in settings where echocardiography is out of reach. Alongside it I work on multimodal foundation models that learn shared representations across ECG, PPG and other clinical signals.

01

Research

Six threads run through my work. They share a question: what does it take for a model trained on physiological signals to remain trustworthy outside the dataset it was fitted on?

  1. Congenital heart disease screening from heart sounds

    The core of my thesis. Convolutional and attention-based models that detect structural heart defects in children directly from phonocardiograms, validated on a cohort of 751 paediatric subjects recorded at four auscultation sites.

    phonocardiography · 1D-CNN · attention transformers · paediatric cardiology

  2. Multimodal foundation models for physiological data

    Representations learned jointly across modalities rather than one signal at a time. CardioFM pretrains over ECG and PPG together; Clin-JEPA extends joint-embedding predictive pretraining to electronic health record trajectories.

    self-supervised learning · JEPA · ECG · PPG · representation learning

  3. How little signal is enough

    Deployment is bounded by recording quality and length, not by architecture. I study signal-quality criteria and minimum segment duration — establishing that five seconds of adequately clean audio supports reliable classification, where three does not.

    signal quality · RMSSD · zero-crossing rate · short-segment inference

  4. Screening where echocardiography is unavailable

    Echocardiography is the diagnostic standard and is inaccessible to much of the world. I work on models that survive the conditions that actually obtain in low-resource screening: a single auscultation site, ambient noise, and recordings a cardiologist would call non-diagnostic.

    global health · digital stethoscope · low-resource deployment

  5. Physiomarker extraction and interpretation

    Tooling that derives cardiorespiratory physiomarkers from raw biosignals reproducibly, and pairs them with language-model interpretation so that clinicians can interrogate what a measurement means rather than accept a number.

    physiomarkers · reproducible pipelines · LLM interpretation

  6. Fairness and transfer across sites

    A model that works in one hospital on one population is not yet a clinical tool. This thread examines demographic bias in medical imaging models and whether learned representations transfer across institutions without added capacity.

    algorithmic fairness · domain shift · cross-site generalisation
